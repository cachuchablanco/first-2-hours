# Injects

Read these to the room. Do not hand out the whole pack first.

No malware sample. No payload. What a clinic would actually see.

## Inject 1 — 07:40 · Discovery

Front desk: "Scheduling won't open. Two other PCs in reception have a full-screen note I don't want to read out loud. Phones still work."

IT is on site. Director is in a meeting. First patients at the door.

**Watch for:** who gets called first, reboots, whether the note gets photographed or clicked.

## Inject 2 — 07:55 · Not one PC

Charge nurse: "Nurses' station is slow. Shared drive with downtime forms is spinning. EHR is stuck on loading. Billing has the same note."

**Watch for:** isolate vs pulling the whole internet, VPN off, one dirty box kept vs wipe-all.

## Inject 3 — 08:15 · Care is the incident too

Director is in the room. A patient is at the desk for a lab draw. Paper forms are in a binder last restocked last year. Nobody is sure the med list printouts are current.

**Watch for:** Director vs IT on stay-open, Charge RN owning paper, someone promising "20 minutes" with no basis.

## Inject 4 — 08:35 · Backup and a bad idea

NAS job shows success at 02:12. NAS is still on the same network as the workstations. Weekly USB is in the IT backpack, last used "a few weeks ago," about to get plugged in "to see if the files are there." A well-meaning person has searched for a decryptor on their phone.

**Watch for:** stop the USB, do not treat NAS as clean, call the EHR vendor, "successful job" ≠ "known-good restore."

## Inject 5 — 09:10 · Outside world

A reporter DMs the clinic Facebook: "Hearing you're closed because of a cyber attack." Billing asks if they should email yesterday's patients. Insurance voicemail: "Do not hire a random firm before you call us."

**Watch for:** one comms owner, no patient email in hour two, no comment to press, insurance before a cold-call IR firm.

## Optional hard mode

EHR is on-prem and the app server has the same note. NAS restore is "maybe tonight" and untested. USB copy is 19 days old.
